Quick-start guide

Getting started with Verve Maths

Verve Maths turns a photo of any maths worksheet into self-marking homework that explains every mistake and shows you exactly what your class understands. This guide walks you through setting up your class and sending your first homework.

1 · Your account

Create your own free teacher account with a username, your name, an email and a four-digit PIN that you choose. (The email is only used to recover your login if you forget it.) Once you've created it, you'll land on the Teacher dashboard.

A quick word on privacyUse pupils' first names only inside Verve, and please don't photograph worksheets that have pupil names or other personal details printed on them.

2 · Set up your class

Give every pupil their own login so Verve can track what each child understands. You can create a whole class in under a minute.

1

Open Students

From the Teacher dashboard, select Students. The first time, the list will be empty.

2

Generate a whole class

Select the pink button “Generate a whole class at once”.

3

Choose how many, then Generate

Enter the number of pupils and select Generate list. Verve creates a unique, easy-to-type username (e.g. robin42) and a four-digit PIN for each pupil.

4

Add names and download the list

Type each child's name into the Name column. Select Download CSV to save the list of usernames and PINs — keep this safe and use it to hand each pupil their login.

5

Submit

Select “Submit — add these students to my account.” The pupils are now on your account and can sign in with their username and PIN.

Keep the CSVThe download is the only copy of the PINs — for security, PINs are stored scrambled and can't be shown again later. You can re-download a list of usernames and names any time from the Students page, but if a PIN is lost you'll simply re-create that pupil to set a new one.

3 · Set your first homework

1

Start a new homework

From the Teacher dashboard, select Set homework, then New homework.

2

Add the worksheet

Take a photo of the worksheet, or Choose photos already on your device. You can add several pages (up to six). See the photo tips below for the best results.

3

Let Verve read it

Select Read questions with AI. Verve turns the worksheet into interactive, self-marking questions.

4

Check and publish

Review each question: check the wording was read correctly and the answer is right — edit anything that needs it — then select Publish homework. Anything Verve is unsure about is flagged for you to confirm.

5

Share with your class

On the final screen you'll get a QR code and a link. Put the QR code on the whiteboard — pupils scan it with a tablet or phone camera, open the homework and sign in with their login. You can also copy the link to share with parents.

Getting the best from the camera

Verve reads the worksheet from your photo, so a clear image makes a big difference to how accurately the questions and answers come through. A few seconds spent on the photo saves checking time later.

Do
  • Fill the frame with one page. Get close so the worksheet fills the photo, with just a small margin around it — one page per photo reads far better than two pages at once.
  • Shoot straight down. Hold the camera flat and directly above the page, not at an angle, so lines and columns stay square. Line the page edges up with the sides of your screen.
  • Use bright, even light. Natural daylight or a well-lit room is ideal. Even lighting across the whole page helps every question read consistently.
  • Flatten the page. Smooth out folds and curl; press a stapled or bent sheet flat. Curved pages distort the text nearest the fold.
  • Keep it sharp. Hold steady and let the camera focus until the text is crisp — tap the screen on the worksheet to focus if your camera allows.
  • Check it's readable before continuing. If you can read every question and number easily at a glance, so can Verve.
Avoid
  • Glare and shadows. Move so window light or a lamp isn't reflecting off the paper, and keep your own shadow (and the phone's) off the page. Shiny spots hide the text underneath.
  • Angled or distant shots. Photos taken from the side, or from too far away, make numbers and symbols harder to read correctly.
  • Cramped crops. Don't cut off the edges of any question — leave a small border so nothing is lost.
  • Very faint or messy print. Faded photocopies and heavy handwriting are the hardest to read; a cleaner original gives the best result.
You always have the final sayVerve does the heavy lifting, but AI isn't perfect — that's why you review every question before publishing. Anything it's not confident about is flagged for you to check, so nothing reaches a pupil until you're happy with it.
